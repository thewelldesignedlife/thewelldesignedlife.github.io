About me

I am a licensed Early-Childhood Educator with 8 years of classroom-based experience.

I seek to translate the rich insights into design and learning that have come from my classroom experience. Some key components of effective learning for me are playfulness, deliberate practice, and respect for the developmental strengths and needs of the learner.

The Early Childhood Lens · 8 years in early childhood classrooms

My years as an Early Childhood Educator forged a deep and practiced understanding of how to design, adapt, and refine learning to be more universally accessible.

Here's how early childhood influences show up in my design:

  • learning goals defined with the learner in mind
  • pre-embedded scaffolding in anticipation of learner support needs
  • applying a developmental lens to meet the needs of every learner (multiple means of engagement, representation, and expression; CAST, 2018)
  • strategically making difficulty desirable, without compromising the learning (Bjork, 1994)

Philosophy

I see design as a non-linear process of moving from “fuzzy encounters” to clarity. “Fuzzy encounters” are the moments we encounter the cues and clues that suggest a friction point in need of design intervention. Engaging with them from a design thinking lens can allow us to move toward clarity. And of course, as the process unfolds, new questions emerge, conditions change, and the fuzz emerges again. We reach clarity by staying engaged in the process, with the humble recognition that good design takes time. We get to “good enough,” and iterate, test, and refine anew when circumstances allow, or the situation demands it.

What I mean when I say “design thinking lens”:

  1. Empathizewith self and/or others
  2. DefineWhat is the friction? How much does it matter? What could be driving it?
  3. Ideatemany possible ways forward
  4. Prototypea quick, rough version to try
  5. Test and refinetry it, learn, adjust.

After Hasso Plattner Institute of Design at Stanford (2010)
